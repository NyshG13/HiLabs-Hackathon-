1. Problem and scope

Scope: diagnosis and condition terms only: 5,005 of the 9,646 labelled rows, 2,305 ICD-10-CM and 2,700 SNOMED CT. Input: only the term and its chunk text. Output: one pre-coordinated code per system, always taken from the supplied dictionaries, so we never emit an illegal code. The two systems are predicted independently; neither is derived from the other through a crosswalk. Nothing reads the subconcept column at prediction time, because the evaluation file will not have it.

Evaluation design: an 80/10/10 split grouped by chunk (seed 13), so that no chart text appears in two parts. The internal test split was scored once, blind; those are the headline numbers. After that, every change was judged on pooled dev+test with 5 chunk-grouped folds and a 20-seed stacker, under a rule declared in advance: keep a change only if accuracy rises, fixed − broken ≥ √(fixed + broken), and unseen-code accuracy drops by no more than 1.5 points. The 95% bar is far above what we reach (about 70%); sections 4 and 5 explain where the remaining 30% goes.

2. Approach and why

3 · DecideLightGBM lambdarank, 20 seeds: an ICD-only stacker, and a shared stacker for SNOMED. SNOMED rows with p < 0.6 (37%) are re-checked by Qwen2.5-1.5B (log p + 0.25 · LLM log-prob)final out-of-fold 74.9 / 67.5
  • Hybrid retrieval, because recall is the ceiling. BM25 and TF-IDF catch exact and rare wording, where embeddings are weak; SapBERT, fine-tuned on training terms, catches paraphrases ("dry heaved" → vomiting). With ≤ 64 candidates the gold is retrieved 94% (ICD) and 90% (SNOMED) of the time. But the fused ranking is right only 41–48% of the time, so the decision has to come later.
  • Cross-encoders read the term in its context. A BiomedBERT cross-encoder sees the term, an anchored window of the chunk, and each candidate's description and hierarchy. It is trained listwise (1 gold vs 7 hard negatives, plus synthetic noisy variants), and this is what resolves collisions like MS → multiple sclerosis vs mitral stenosis. It is the best single model (52–56%), yet still far from the final number.
  • A learned stacker, not hand-written rules, sets the specificity policy. We add the "unspecified" sibling, the laterality variants and the 7th-character variants into the pool. Features (unspecified flag, depth, billable, laterality/acuity conflict, extra or missing words, code priors) then let LightGBM learn when the coders choose E11.9 over a complication code. Combining ~55 signals adds +23 points (ICD) and +12 points (SNOMED) over the best single model; averaging 20 seeds removes about ±1 point of seed noise.
  • Mining local convention. A convention kNN lets the nearest labelled training terms vote for codes (ICD +1.5). A training-term memory recognises terms the coders have already labelled.
  • LLMs only where judgement is needed. Qwen2.5-1.5B re-checks only low-confidence SNOMED picks (37% of rows; +2.0 points). It is deterministic: one forward pass with no sampling. It is off for ICD, where it hurt. Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B is used zero-shot as one more ICD feature (+1.1).
  • Everything is local and open-weight. No data leaves the machine, as the data-use rules require.

5. Error analysis: where it still fails, and why

failure mode (blind test errors)ICD (67)SNOMED (81)examples (term → gold · our prediction)
Gold never retrieved (a recall ceiling)11 (16%)22 (27%)"asalr" (unexpanded abbreviation); "atypicals" → antipsychotic agent; "critically ill" → Patient's condition poor

Wrong concept36 (54%)59 (73%)two conditions in one term: "asthma, uncontrolled t2dm"; combination code: "CKD … hypertension" → I12.9 · N18.9
  • Negation and context. SNOMED codes negated findings as "normal/absent" concepts: "acute changes" → No status change, and "acute ischemic ECG changes" → ECG no myocardial ischemia, because the chunk negates them. Our term-centred scorers miss this. A negation-alignment feature did not help (−0.6 ICD).
  • SNOMED granularity and semantic tags. Accuracy is 76% on disorder and 69% on finding concepts, but only 44% on observable entity, 30% on morphologic abnormality and 19% on situation. In 97 of 176 out-of-fold SNOMED errors the prediction has the right semantic tag but the wrong concept. In other words, the coders' tag convention (finding vs morphology vs disorder) is only partly learnable from 2,700 labelled rows.
  • ICD chapters. The weakest chapter is musculoskeletal (M, 58%, n=59), followed by L (69%) and Z (71%); C, K and E are 87–100%. The large symptom chapter R (n=149) sits at 74%, mostly on catch-all codes such as R29.898 and R68.89.
  • Label noise sets a ceiling. We hand-audited 100 picking misses. In 38–43% of them the gold label was arguable or the prediction equally valid: near-duplicate SNOMED concepts, ICD headers or older-release codes versus valid FY2026 leaves, and identical training text carrying the predicted code. Another 43–60% were learnable conventions, and ≤ 13% needed context.
  • The gate is a double-edged sword. In 17 of 119 SNOMED picking misses, Qwen2.5 overrode a correct stacker pick, even though it is a net +2 points.

Plan for the remaining gap, in order of expected gain: (1) Recall: context-conditioned abbreviation expansion and the CDC index lookup, which are already built as features but not yet evaluated, plus larger SNOMED pools. These target the 16–27% of errors that are never retrieved. (2) SNOMED long tail: ship the in-domain reranker that already passed the keep rule (+0.9), and add the SNOMED IS-A hierarchy for granularity decisions. (3) Specificity as an explicit decision: choose the 3-character category first (already 81.5% right), then descend. (4) Combination codes and two-condition terms: rules for known pairs (e.g. I12.x for hypertension with CKD). (5) Release drift: map older-release and header golds to FY2026 leaves using the CDC addenda, and rebuild the dictionaries automatically each October and twice a year for SNOMED. (6) A gate trained to predict when overriding helps, and abstention: routing the 40–50% of low-confidence terms to review gives 90%-precision automatic coding today.

6. Open questions: our positions

  • Partial credit or strict? The headline should stay strict exact match, and we report it that way. Partial credit should be published alongside it, because it separates very different failures: our ICD exact is 72.4% but the category is right 81.5% of the time, so nearly a third of ICD errors are specificity, not misunderstanding. For SNOMED we would need the IS-A relationships to compute hierarchy distance.
  • Ambiguous gold answers? In the data, no (term, chunk, system) carries two golds, so we score strictly against the single gold. Our audit shows real ambiguity, though: near-duplicate SNOMED concepts (coronary atherosclerosis vs arteriosclerosis), and header or older-release ICD codes. We propose accepting a small, adjudicated set of equivalent codes for such pairs, reported as a lenient secondary metric so the strict headline stays comparable.
  • More than one annotator? We could not measure inter-annotator agreement: each row has one gold. Our audit suggests the practical ceiling is clearly below 100%. We recommend double-annotating a few hundred evaluation rows and publishing agreement as the ceiling; otherwise 95% may sit above what annotators themselves agree on.
  • Per-category minimums? Yes, with a minimum sample size per category (e.g. chapters with ≥ 30 rows), since small chapters are noisy. Our per-chapter report shows what a floor would catch: chapter M at 58% versus E/K above 87%.

7. Engineering, reproducibility, and external resources

Engineering: git clone (trained weights come via Git LFS) → pip install -r requirements.txt → python setup_model.py (verifies the sha256 of all weights and downloads the two Qwen models at pinned revisions) → python predict.py --input eval.csv --output predictions.csv.

  • Deterministic: fixed seeds, and the LLMs run without sampling. Two fresh runs are byte-identical, and a fresh clone reproduced the reference predictions on 986/989 ICD and 989/989 SNOMED codes; the 3 differences are near-ties that flip with GPU half-precision rounding.
  • Robust input handling: an input guard drops any extra column, and make test proves the output is unchanged.
  • Safe: no API keys exist; .env is git-ignored. score.py reproduces every number above from the submitted predictions.
  • Deployability: 3.9 s per new term on a 4 GB laptop GPU is the honest figure. Most of it is cross-encoder scoring of about 70 candidates and the Qwen3 pass, which batch well on a server GPU. A CPU-only mode without Qwen3 keeps ICD at 73.8%.
